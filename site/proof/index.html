ML→close = to-1 points the price moved from the morning line to post. Positive means it shortened. This is descriptive only: because your payout is the final price regardless of when you bet, our “play at” figure is a reference price and not a rule. There is no earlier price to catch and nothing to hold you to. To be unambiguous, because it matters: a flag that closes below that figure is still graded on the public record, win or lose. It does not become a pass after the fact. A run of “short” closes is a sign our number was set against a loose morning line, not the real market.

Our call is an early hold/shorten read set from each flag’s committed board price. Long-priced flags the crowd tends to leave alone hold, short ones get bet down. It is a heuristic on a small sample, shown next to what actually happened so you can watch it prove out or fail in public. Not a bet-timing tip: you are paid the closing price whenever you bet, so a flag we call “likely bet down” is simply a likely pass. The read is versioned: cards locked before August 24, 2026 carry the call they were published with, from one pair of thresholds used at both meets, and cards from that date use thresholds set separately for each track. This column therefore mixes two versions on purpose. A call we have already published is not restated.

Brier score: lower is better (0 = perfect). Ours and the morning line’s sit within a thousandth of each other, and that is the honest headline: a standalone Brier comparison cannot tell a real model from a plausible one. Both numbers are dominated by the fact that most horses lose, and a model that did nothing but copy the board would score the same as the board.

So we ask the question that separates them: does our number add anything the board does not already contain? Fit the outcome on the market’s estimate alone, then again with ours added, and test whether the second fit is genuinely better.

The answer is suggestive, at the edge of significance, on a sample too small to lean on. We are publishing all of it rather than the flattering part.

The test was chosen and written down before we ran it, by someone other than the person who built the model. That matters more than the result. Anyone can fit models until one of them clears a threshold and then publish that one; the only defence is to fix the method in advance and be bound by whatever it returns. The estimator below, a conditional logit on race choice sets, was specified, written and frozen by a separate reviewer before any of these numbers existed, and we are reporting exactly what it produced, including the two checks that go against us.

Frozen analysis, as of 2026-07-31. Every figure in this section is pinned to the ledger as it stood that day and is deliberately not re-fit as new races are graded. Re-fitting a pre-registered test to newer data is the thing pre-registration exists to prevent. The race and runner counts below are therefore smaller than the calibration counts above, and that is expected, not an error. When it is re-run, it will be re-run in full and dated again.

The pre-registered test is a conditional logit over race choice sets, 138 races, 1,170 runners. Our estimate carries a coefficient of +0.6643 (standard error 0.3013) once the board is already in the model, likelihood-ratio χ²(1) = 4.833, p = 0.0279.

Two robustness checks disagree with that, and they matter more than the headline. Allowing for the fact that horses in the same race are not independent gives a cluster-robust p of 0.0548, the wrong side of the conventional 0.05 line. And when whole races are resampled and the test refit, 41% of those refits fail to reach significance at all. A result that disappears two times in five under resampling is not a finding to build on.

An earlier version of this page reported p = 0.026 from the same data. That number was overstated and we are correcting it. It treated all 1,181 runners as independent observations. They are not: exactly one horse wins each race, and the probabilities are worked out relative to the others in that race, so the real number of independent units is the 138 races, not the runners. Ignoring that makes any result look stronger than it is.

We are deliberately not printing a yes or no here. The method and every number are published together so you can weigh them yourself, and the full output is in the machine-readable record. Judge it again at the end of the meet, when there is more of it.

Corrections & verification

Two integrity defects were found on 2026-07-26 and are recorded here in full. No card, metadata file, ledger entry, recorded hash, or timestamp proof was rewritten to make either go away.

How to verify a card

Download card.json from any card page and hash it (sha256sum card.json). That value should equal the downloadable artifact hash shown in that card’s “Verifying this card” box. The card’s OpenTimestamps proof commits to those same bytes.

There are four independent evidence layers, answering different questions:

  1. The append-only ledger and Git history: when the card was committed, and that nothing was edited afterwards.
  2. The card lock and publication timestamps: that the card existed before first post.
  3. The OpenTimestamps calendar commitment: independent third-party evidence that those exact bytes existed when we say they did.
  4. A Bitcoin attestation, once the proof is upgraded: the same evidence anchored in a block.

Layers 1 and 2 establish that a pick was published before post. A Bitcoin confirmation arriving later strengthens the external evidence; it is not what makes the pre-post claim true, and a pending proof does not weaken it.

Card hashes: recorded lock hash vs downloadable file

Cards published before 2026-07-26 were generated on Windows. The lock hash was calculated from CRLF-formatted bytes before Git stored the same JSON with LF line endings, so the recorded hash does not match the file you can download. The selections and JSON values are unchanged. The difference is exclusively line endings, proven byte-for-byte for every affected card. This broke reproducible verification, which is why it is disclosed here rather than described as a formatting detail.

Provenance display defects

Cases where the evidence was intact but the site did not show it. Recorded to the same standard as anything else here: what a reader saw, what actually caused it, and how long it lasted.

PD-2026-08-01-01 · 4 cards

Affected: 2026-07-31-dmr, 2026-07-31-sar, 2026-08-01-dmr, 2026-08-01-sar
Window: approximately two days; began when the 07-31 cards published, ending 2026-08-01T18:05:00+00:00
Found by: us, from a failing test

What readers saw. These four card pages rendered with no 'Verifying this card' block: no downloadable artifact hash and no OpenTimestamps status. Cards from 07-25 and 07-26 displayed theirs normally, so the omission read as arbitrary rather than as a build stage that had not run.

Why. The site build rendered pages BEFORE regenerating proof_status.json, and it writes that file into the same tree it reads its inputs from. A build on a clean checkout therefore rendered from whatever proof_status.json happened to be committed, and the committed copy covered 17 of 21 cards -- it did not yet list these four. The renderer found no proof entry for them and emitted no verification block. The build then overwrote the stale file, so a second build produced different bytes from the first.

The proofs were never missing. Not a missing, invalid, expired or mis-targeted proof, and not a hash discrepancy. No card, ledger entry, recorded hash or timestamp proof was altered at any point. Every affected card held a valid timestamp proof committing to its current published bytes throughout.

Effect on the record: No selection, result, grade or published figure changed. The defect was entirely in what the page displayed about verification.

Fixed. scripts/build_deployable_site.py now regenerates record.json, proof_status.json and proof_registry.json BEFORE rendering. Verified from a genuinely clean checkout -- site/ and all three regenerated files deleted -- that two consecutive builds differ in 0 of 208 files and all four cards render their block on the first build.

Still open. The build still writes into its own source tree, which is the property that made this possible. Making the archive root read-only input is scheduled and recorded in docs/known_issues/build_writes_into_its_own_source_tree.md.

The card hash embedded in the metadata never changed, and the card.json proofs are unaffected. Metadata is presentation, not locked evidence. The card is the locked evidence.
